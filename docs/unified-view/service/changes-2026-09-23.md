# Service Work — the 2026-09-23 change list, analysed

Seventeen requested changes. **They are not seventeen pieces of work.** Two are
already built, five are field-level, four are one new idea wearing four hats,
five belong to a product this repo does not have yet, and one is a new surface
somewhere else entirely.

This file says what each one touches, what it costs, and **the five things
nobody has decided yet** — because three of those block work that would
otherwise be quick.

## 3. The one new idea, wearing four hats: **WHO**

| # | Asked for |
|---|---|
| 5 | Start work can be initiated by a dispatcher too |
| 9 | A quote can be sent by a dispatcher too |
| 12 | Sales people can edit work orders |
| 16b | An automatic invoice is editable by sales or dispatch |

**This module has no idea who is looking at it.** Today "who can do what" is
encoded in a *setting* — `techApp` on or off — not in a *person*. With the
switch on, the order sheet deliberately **cannot** complete a job, on the
grounds that "the person holding the job is standing in front of the asset".

**All four of these contradict that.** Taken together they say one thing:

> **The field app is where the technician works. It is not the only place the
> work can be recorded.**

That is a **reversal of the 2026-09-21 decision**, not an extension of it, and
it should be taken deliberately rather than arrived at by implementing four
tickets. See decision D3.

**The cheap version and the real version.** The cheap version is to drop the
lock: dispatch can do everything the technician can, and the field app becomes
a convenience rather than a gate. That is a day's work and satisfies all four
lines as written. The real version is a role model — dispatcher, sales,
technician, manager — with a viewer switch, which is a week and changes every
screen in the module. **Nothing in the seventeen actually asks for the second
one**; #12 asks only that sales can edit.

## 4. The technician app — a separate product

| # | Asked for |
|---|---|
| 6 | Built out separately |
| 7 | An **assessment** section, once the work starts |
| 8 | A **Skip** beside *Send quote to customer* |
| 15 | **Notes**, after work-complete and after parts-pending |

Plus the standing instruction: **make it a mobile flow of its own.**

### What it is today

`TechnicianApp.tsx` — a 400px-wide panel that opens *over the dispatch sheet*,
with its own header, deliberately unlike the rest of the app. Four steps:
arrive → quote → waiting → work.

### What "separate" costs

1. **Its own destination**, not a panel over a sheet. It stops being something
   dispatch opens and becomes somewhere you are.
2. **Its own shell.** The app shell — rail, drawer, top bar — is a desktop
   shell. A phone product does not get a 56px rail.
3. **A new fixture**: the technician's own job list. Today it is handed one job
   because dispatch picked it.

### The thing to say out loud first

**The DS has no mobile rules. None.** No touch target minimum, no thumb-reach
rule, no phone layout, no rule for a full-screen step versus a sheet. Every
desktop rule in `docs/ds/` assumes a pointer and a wide viewport.

So building this **forces the DS's first mobile surface**, and there are only
two ways to do that:

- **Write the rules first** — a short mobile section in the DS: targets,
  reach, one-thing-per-screen, how a step ends. Then build against it.
- **Build first and harvest** — build the app, then promote what settled.

The second is how Service Work and 3PL were both built and it worked, **but
both had a desktop DS underneath them to borrow from. This one has nothing.**
Recommendation: a thin set of mobile rules first — half a day, not a week —
then build. See decision D5.

### #7 and #8 are cheap once the app exists

- **Assessment** is a step between *arrive* and *work*: what the technician
  found, which is not the same as what was reported. It is also the first thing
  in this module that would make the fault field honest — `fault` is what the
  customer said, and nothing currently records what was true.
- **Skip** beside *Send quote* is one button, and it matters: it says out loud
  that not every job is quoted, which the current flow only expresses by
  turning a setting off.
- **Notes** after complete and after parts-pending: two text areas, and they
  should be one field on the record with a timestamp, not two.

## 6. The new surface

| # | Change |
|---|---|
| 17 | Service work orders attached as history on the ASSET |

**The asset record has no service history today** — it shows next-service-due
and a maintenance status, and nothing about what has actually been done to it.
This is a table on `AssetRecord`, and it is the first thing in the app that
makes the asset a subject rather than a field on an order.

**It is also the most independently valuable item on the list** and depends on
none of the others. Good candidate to do early.

## The five decisions

**D1 — which section is "the billing section"?** There is no section by that
name. Candidates: *Fees*, *Additional charges*, the invoice rungs, or the money
sections as a group. *Blocks #3.*

**D2 — labour: computed, or typed?** #10 says it is computed from start→complete.
#2 says its input is a text box. Both cannot be plainly true. Three options:
computed and read-only; computed with an explicit override; or typed, with the
timestamps only as a suggestion. **The DS rule is that a derived value is a
read-out and never a disabled field**, so option two needs an edit affordance,
not a greyed box. *Blocks #2 and #10.*

**D3 — is the field-app lock dropped?** Four changes say dispatch can do what
the technician does. Confirm the reversal, and confirm the cheap version
(dispatch can do everything) rather than a full role model. *Blocks #5, #9, #12,
#16b.*

**D4 — where does approval go?** Today: In progress → Needs approval → Approved
→ Completed, with `managerApproval` able to skip the middle. If a **Completed**
button marks the job complete, is Completed the technician's submit (approval
still follows), or does the ladder collapse? *Blocks #13.*

**D5 — mobile rules before the app, or after?** Recommendation: a thin set
first, because there is no mobile precedent anywhere in the DS to borrow.

---

## Suggested order

1. **#17 asset service history** — independent, valuable, blocked by nothing.
2. **#1, #4, #11** — the field changes that no decision blocks.
3. **D1–D4, then #2, #3, #10, #13** — the rest of the dispatch sheet.
4. **D5, then the mobile rules.**
5. **The technician app as its own flow**, with #7, #8, #15 built into it
   rather than bolted on.

**#6 last is deliberate.** The app is the biggest piece and the one whose
requirements are least settled; everything before it makes the record it works
against correct first.
